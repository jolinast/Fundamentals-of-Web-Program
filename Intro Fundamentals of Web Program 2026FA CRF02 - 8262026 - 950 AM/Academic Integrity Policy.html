Do your own work

Here are common reasons why the instructor may give zero points on an assignment for an academic integrity violation.

  1. A student submits an assignment with code that has not been taught in class.
    • This is usually an indication that the student used artificial intelligence or a friend for help. 
    • To earn credit, the student must document the untaught line(s) of code with comments explaining its purpose.
    • The teacher may require the student to record a video demonstrating their logic and explaining their work.
  2. A student who has previously turned in low-quality work turns in perfect work.
    • Again, this is usually an indication that the student used artificial intelligence or a friend for help.
    • The teacher may require the student to record a video demonstrating their logic and explaining their work.
  3. Two or more students turn in identical code with identical errors. 
    • While it's possible for two students to develop the same solution, it is not likely that they would also have the same typographical errors.
    • The instructor may require the students to complete an alternative assignment.
  4. A student turns in multiple assignments within a short period of time.
    • Completing two or more assignments within 10 minutes of each other, for example, usually indicates that the student used artificial intelligence or a friend for help.
    • Completing two or more CodeLab challenges within less than one minute of each other, for example, usually indicates that the student found solutions online and is copying and pasting answers.
    • The teacher may require the student to record a video demonstrating their logic and explaining their work.

Artificial Intelligence

The use of artificial intelligence tools is discouraged for all assignments. Having an AI generate any of the design documents or code would only show the skill of that application, not the skill of the student.

Please note that some instructors prohibit the use of AI completely. The instructor for this class recognizes the following uses of AI as acceptable:

  • Explaining code samples from the textbook or written by the instructor. The AI may write comments in the code to help you know what each line is doing.
  • Identifying errors. Rather than spending hours troubleshooting a problem, ask an AI tool or the instructor what is wrong and how to fix it.
  • Being a study partner who helps you think deeply, not just find answers. Instead of asking for the solution, prompt the AI to ask questions and encourage reflection—making learning more personal, engaging, and effective.
  • Brainstorming a project idea that you are passionate about. You could even prompt an AI tool to generate code for a game that is unrelated to class, like Hangman, Mastermind, Wordle, or the Snake Game.
  • After completing and turning in an assignment, ask an AI tool to clean up the code to make it more efficient. Don't turn this code in for grading, but use it to learn best practices.
